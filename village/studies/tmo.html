Search & Navigation

One search for shopping and support

T-Mobile · Senior UX Designer, Lead · Feb 2020 to Sept 2021

A search for a phone could mean buying one, finding an accessory, or getting help with the one you already owned. I joined T-Mobile's search redesign mid-project as the only designer on a three-person team. I led design and research direction across web and mobile. The search redesign increased usage 24%, reduced zero-result searches 59%, and increased phone sales 11%.

Help people form a useful query

The schedule left little room for a fresh discovery phase. I worked with the research team on known search behavior, reviewed Clicktale heatmaps and query analytics, and defined questions for the in-house researchers and external vendors. That let me work from existing evidence while the design moved forward.

I designed the experience before and during typing: recent searches and popular terms gave people a starting point, then suggestions connected what they typed to available content. Suggestions included categories, so a partial query could lead toward a phone or toward support before someone reached the results page.

The middle screens show the key transition: a partial “iPh” query offers both phones and support. The results view carries those categories forward.

Suggestions took space in a small viewport, especially with the keyboard open. I kept the category cues close to the query and the suggested results, so people could make that choice without opening another control.

Keep buying and getting help equally reachable

I carried the distinction into the results: phones and devices, accessories, and support, with an “All” view for people who hadn't narrowed their intent. Product results showed images and prices. Support results used article titles and descriptions. Each result type needed enough context to tell someone whether it was useful.

Category tabs let people change direction while keeping the query. Filters then narrowed the results within that direction. On mobile, I kept the applied filters visible as removable chips, with the fuller set of controls in a filter panel. People could see and undo their choices without reopening the panel.

The same query across product and support views. Category tabs change the kind of result; chips expose the filters already applied. The panel holds the remaining filter controls.

Acknowledge the gap for Sprint customers

The merger left another way for a useful search to miss its destination: someone could still need Sprint content. I added “Looking for Sprint results?” beside the results, with a direct link to Sprint search.

That introduced a route out of the experience, but it gave people an explicit next step when the content they needed lived elsewhere. It also made a real product limitation visible instead of implying the results covered everything.

The Sprint link sits above the product results, where someone can find it without clearing their query or digging through navigation.
