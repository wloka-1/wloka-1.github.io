Zero to One

Make the market understandable before the trade

Overlay Protocol · Co-Founder, COO & Head of Design · Sept 2021 to Feb 2025

Overlay let people trade on niche data markets. The interface needed to explain what was available before asking someone to take a position. As co-founder, COO, and head of design, I owned the product experience, brand, and component library. Across four app iterations, I moved the entry experience from trade controls to browsable markets, then featured markets. The team shipped the product and grew from 3 to 25 through a $4M seed round.

Put the markets before the trading controls

The early interface opened with a market, long and short controls, leverage, and an amount. That was a direct route for someone who already knew what they wanted to trade. It did little to explain why they should be there.

I improved the legibility, but people still arrived confused. The next change was structural: make the first screen a list of available markets, with names, prices, and categories people could scan before opening a trade. Later, featured markets gave the more unusual offerings a visible place in that experience.

From left to right: trade controls dominate the early screens; the later designs start with market discovery. The change was what people encountered first, as well as how it looked.

This added a selection step before trading. I made that tradeoff to give people a chance to understand the available markets. The screens show the change; I don't have a measured onboarding improvement to claim for it.

The shipped desktop interface carries the market-first direction into a larger viewport.

Keep a direct line to people using it

I set up small core-user groups in Telegram and used Discord discussions and polls to collect feedback as we built. I could ask about a specific interaction, hear what had gone wrong, and follow up with the same person.

A user reports that building a position worked, then raises gas costs. My reply discusses a possible network move and a bridge that still needed testing.

That exchange kept the product discussion grounded: a working interface could still be expensive to use. It also shows the limit of this research. These were engaged community members willing to talk to a founder. Their feedback was useful, but it couldn't stand in for people who never understood the product well enough to join the conversation.

Give the brand personality and the controls consistent behavior

I wrote the design ethos around “scientists and cats.” The protocol had technical depth; the brand could still be playful. I developed the early identity and later brought in a brand agency as it matured.

Inside the app, I built a shared component library with our front-end lead. It covered the interaction states as well as the look: buttons waiting on an action, selected tabs, open dropdowns, and headers at different widths. I kept developer notes beside the components so we could resolve the behavior where the work lived.

The library defines pending, inactive, and selected states alongside responsive headers. That gave new features a shared interaction vocabulary.

The library grew alongside the product. It let us carry the visual language into referrals, staking, and gamification without deciding every control again.

Founder scope also meant hiring, operations, and investor relationships. Our social audiences reached 77K on Twitter and 50K on Discord. Those audiences could overlap; they aren't a count of active traders.
